Infotainment center technology got its rudimentary start with the automobile radio. In the early twentieth century, radios began appearing in automobiles, giving drivers access to music and news while traveling. As technology improved, AM and later FM radio became standard features in many vehicles. During the 1970s and 1980s, cassette players became common, followed by CD players in the 1990s.

The 2000s saw the introduction of satellite radio, which provided drivers with a wider range of channels and programming options. In recent years, infotainment centers have become increasingly sophisticated, incorporating touchscreens, voice recognition, and smartphone integration. Today, many vehicles come equipped with advanced infotainment systems that offer a wide range of features, including navigation, entertainment, and connectivity options.

The development of digital technology dramatically changed vehicle infotainment. Manufacturers began adding digital displays, GPS navigation, satellite radio, Bluetooth connectivity, and USB ports. In the 2000s and 2010s, touchscreen displays became increasingly common and began combining many functions into one system.

The integration of auxiliary (AUX) ports, early Bluetooth hands-free profiles, and built-in hard-drive GPS systems (such as early iterations of BMW's iDrive or Ford's SYNC) marked the birth of modern infotainment. Dashboards began incorporating small, often monochrome or low-resolution color screens.
